An online retail extension of Real Tech Electricals, developed from a business opportunity into a custom shopping platform.

Current status

Live — ongoing optimization

The public storefront is accessible. Development and improvements continue. Updated on 6 October 2026 using public pages, the existing portfolio, and the developer's account.

Problem and project origin

According to the developer, the owner of Real Tech Electricals also sells electrical appliances and gadgets. During development of the service platform, the owner suggested offering these products online.

The developer translated that suggestion into EasyShopen: a retail channel that complements the business's installation and technician services. The project began with a business goal rather than a detailed requirements specification.

Customers needed a way to discover products, place orders, and choose fulfilment options without relying solely on visiting the shop.

Contribution

The developer reports independently defining the product concept, structure, workflows, and interface design from the owner's suggestion. The contribution includes full-stack development, database workflows, and ongoing platform improvements.

This demonstrates requirements discovery and solution design: turning a general request to sell online into a structured customer shopping experience.

The relationship to Real Tech is a business extension. Public links between the sites do not establish shared databases, unified accounts, or automatic technical integration.

Scope recorded in the portfolio

The existing portfolio also describes payment integration, administrative reporting, database workflows, and progressive web application development. These remain part of the documented project scope; private administration and payment processing were not exercised during this review.

Relationship to Real Tech Electricals

EasyShopen focuses on product shopping, while Real Tech's platform focuses on booking and coordinating technicians. The public Services page connects shopping with installation support, including television mounting, appliance installation, and electrical work.

This gives the business a way to present both products and related services. It is not evidence that every purchase automatically creates a technician booking.

Nationwide service access is a stated business ambition. Actual delivery options and technician availability depend on location and operational coverage; universal coverage is not claimed here.

Design emphasis

  • Developer-led product definition: the owner supplied the business suggestion; the developer reports designing the solution's structure and workflows.
  • A connected customer journey: product discovery, ordering, fulfilment, and access to related services are presented coherently.
  • Useful product information: customers can inspect items before deciding to order.
  • Continuing improvement: the live platform remains subject to optimization rather than being presented as a finished, unchanging product.

Technology areas

Full-stack web development, relational database workflows, product search and catalogue interfaces, cart and checkout design, customer-account workflows, order tracking, administrative reporting, and progressive web application development.

The general portfolio stack is not assigned wholesale to this project. An install button alone does not establish offline purchasing or synchronization capabilities.
